Judgment at Machine Speed01 · Executive summary
Executive summary

Content is cheap. Judgment is scarce.

Generative AI has moved the constraint on enterprise value. The question is no longer how fast teams can produce, but how well they can evaluate what they produce.

Most organizations deployed AI faster than they built the habits, controls and culture to scrutinize it. The result is a hidden risk position: errors embedded in deliverables, sensitive data flowing into unmanaged tools, and a workforce that distrusts AI yet depends on it.

58%of employees use AI at work regularly

The goal is not frictionless automation. It is the right friction in the right places: machine speed where errors are cheap, human scrutiny where they are not.

04 · The judgment operating model

Build scepticism into the workflow

Scepticism must be built into how work flows, not left to individual temperament. Match the level of review to the cost of an error.

minimum human review

Red-team review

Task-level checksDoes the output meet the standard an expert would expect?

Component-level checksAre sources real, figures traceable, reasoning unbiased?

Adversarial checksProbe for prompt injection and unverified citations.

Judgment at Machine Speed04 · Operating model
Map the assumptions

Test the critical claims with the weakest evidence first

Break a fluent AI recommendation into testable claims: do customers want it, does it pay, can we deliver it, does it survive change? Then score each on importance and evidence.

Importance →

Test first

Critical but weakly evidenced: the leap-of-faith assumptions.

Verify and monitor

Critical and well evidenced: confirm sources, track for change.

Park

Unproven but low stakes: revisit if importance rises.

Accept

Low stakes, well evidenced: no further testing.

Weak evidenceStrong evidence →

Score evidence on what supports a claim, not how it reads. Fluent prose and model confidence count as weak evidence.

Four challenge prompts
01

Stress-tester

Strongest counter-arguments on financial, ethical and practical grounds.

02

Pre-mortem analyst

It is 12 months later and the plan failed. Explain why.

03

Blind-spot detector

Ignore the strengths; list the biases and data gaps.

04

Brutal editor

Flag every unsupported claim and logical gap.

Caution: a model critiquing its own output shares its blind spots. Challenge prompts widen the search; they do not replace checks against primary sources.

05 · Roadmap

Visibility before control

Three phases, each ending at a gate leadership must pass before scaling. Visibility comes first, because controls designed blind push AI use further out of sight.

Days 0–30

Illuminate

Launch a 30-day AI amnesty · Map tools, data flows and demand · Define risk tiers 1–3 · Name an executive sponsor

Gate 1: shadow-use map complete, tiers agreed
Days 30–90

Equip

License secure AI tools · Deploy access and data-loss controls · Train on bias and verification · Pilot red-team review in 2–3 high-risk workflows

Gate 2: pilots catch errors without slowing delivery
Days 90–180

Embed

Scale controls to all tier-3 work · Add psychological safety to manager goals · Set time-reinvestment targets · Review KPIs quarterly

D180

What good looks like: AI use is visible, high-risk outputs are reviewed by a named owner, the KPI scorecard is baselined, and managers are measured on whether people feel safe to challenge AI.
