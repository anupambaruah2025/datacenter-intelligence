Judgment at Machine Speed Executive summary

Content is cheap. Judgment is scarce.

Generative AI has moved the constraint on enterprise value. The question is no longer how fast teams can produce, but how well they can evaluate what they produce.

Most organizations deployed AI faster than they built the habits, controls and culture to scrutinize it. The result is a hidden risk position: errors embedded in deliverables, sensitive data flowing into unmanaged tools, and a workforce that distrusts AI yet depends on it.

58%of employees use AI at work regularly

The goal is not frictionless automation. It is the right friction in the right places: machine speed where errors are cheap, human scrutiny where they are not.

page 04 01 · The situation

Adoption has outrun scrutiny

AI is now embedded in core work, but the verification habits needed to use it safely are not. The gap between use and scrutiny is where enterprise risk accumulates.

The benefits are real. Among employees using AI:

higher efficiency

better access to information

better work quality

The problem is not use. It is how outputs are handled once they arrive.

page 07 The trust paradox

Wary of the tool, dependent on it

Deloitte's TrustID research reports a sharp drop in corporate workers' trust in generative AI between May and July 2025, as people moved from experiments to high-stakes tasks and met the tools' limits.

Fewer than half of users have been trained in responsible use. Falling trust has not produced better checking. It has produced a workforce that doubts AI but relies on it to hit productivity targets. That is the most dangerous combination: the doubt is felt but not acted on.

page 08 02 · Diagnosis

Why capable people stop checking

Poor scrutiny of AI output is a predictable product of human cognition, not negligence. Reminders and warnings underperform because they do not address the mechanism.

<½correct answers with faulty AI support vs. no AI (Reutlingen)

Telling people the AI may be wrong helps. It does not return performance to the unassisted level.

page 09 Three failure modes
AAutomation bias

Deferring to the machine

Faulty AI support more than halved correct answers. A warning nudge helped but did not restore baseline. Self-rated AI literacy gave no protection.

BFluency bias

The confidence illusion

Polished prose reads as evidence. In HBS research, experts and novices were equally persuaded by incorrect AI narratives.

CAI gravity

Cognitive debt

Constant pressure to offload thinking erodes the very skills needed to evaluate the output (MIT Sloan, Eric So).

Two errors managers should name

  • Errors of omissionThe AI did not flag a problem, so no one looked.
  • Errors of commissionThe AI was wrong and someone acted on it anyway.

Implication: controls must change the workflow, forcing independent reasoning before AI contributes or adversarial testing after, rather than relying on people to remember to be sceptical.

page 10 03 · Shadow AI

You cannot govern what you cannot see

Shadow AI is a rational response to friction. Employees use unsanctioned tools to escape slow enterprise systems, cut manual re-keying and keep up with productivity expectations.

57%

Conceal AI use

Hidden usage blinds risk teams.

$670K

Extra breach cost

Average uplift where shadow-AI activity is high, driven by slower detection.

Why bans fail

Prohibition drives use underground and blinds security teams. It also creates a legal double bind: unmanaged use risks confidentiality breaches, while refusing tools that materially improve accuracy may, over time, fall below the expected standard of care.

page 11 The alternative

Amnesty first, guardrails second

  1. 01

    Declare amnesty

    A no-blame window, typically 30 days, to disclose tools, plug-ins and personal subscriptions. Delivers a true map of data flows.

  2. 02

    Analyse demand

    Group disclosures by use case and data sensitivity to expose gaps in the sanctioned toolset.

  3. 03

    Provide secure alternatives

    License enterprise-grade tools that meet the real demand and remove the incentive to work around policy.

  4. 04

    Apply guardrails

    Identity and access controls, data-loss prevention, endpoint monitoring and clear acceptable-use rules.

  5. 05

    Rebuild trust

    Report what was learned and what changed, so transparency is visibly rewarded.

Guardrails imposed before the amnesty simply teach people to hide better.

page 12 04 · Psychological safety

The control layer policy cannot replace

If people fear looking replaceable or foolish, they will neither disclose AI use nor challenge a confident machine.

Amy Edmondson defines psychological safety as a shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk. It is not comfort or lower standards. It is permission, and expectation, to disagree and interrogate ideas.

71%fear permanent
job losses

Identity threat pushes people from learning mode into defensive mode, where silence about AI use and AI errors is the safer personal choice.

page 13 How AI erodes it
  • Identity threatAI automates the analysis many careers were built on.
  • Silence about useAdmitting reliance feels like admitting replaceability.
  • Silence about errorsQuestioning a fluent output risks looking wrong twice.
  • DistanceRemote, asynchronous work reduces informal candour.

What managers can do

01

Model fallibility

Share specific times AI misled you and how you caught it.

02

Separate failure types

Reward learning from low-risk experiments; hold firm on skipped mandatory reviews.

03

Ask for the downside

"What is the worst outcome if this AI-supported recommendation is wrong?"

04

Debate live, disclose freely

Discuss high-stakes AI output synchronously; make stating AI's contribution routine.

Success is behavioural: people flag AI errors early, disclose use as a matter of course, and challenge outputs a senior colleague endorsed.

page 14 05 · Operating model

Build scepticism into the workflow

Tier the work by risk

1Low

Drafts and brainstorming

Self-check; AI use noted.

2Moderate

Internal decisions and reports

Named owner verifies sources and key figures.

3High

Client, financial, legal, safety, strategy

Assumption map plus independent red-team review before release.

Map assumptions: importance × evidence

Importance →

Test first

Critical but weakly evidenced: the leap-of-faith assumptions.

Verify and monitor

Critical and well evidenced: confirm sources, track for change.

Park

Unproven but low stakes: revisit if importance rises.

Accept

Low stakes, well evidenced: no further testing.

Weak evidenceStrong evidence →

Score evidence on what supports a claim, not how it reads. Fluent prose and model confidence count as weak evidence.

page 15 Red team and adversarial prompts
  • Task-level checksDoes the output meet the standard an expert would expect?
  • Component-level checksAre sources real, figures traceable, reasoning unbiased?
  • Adversarial checksProbe for prompt injection and unverified citations.
  • No metric shortcutBLEU or ROUGE can score well on confidently wrong output.

Four challenge personas

01

Stress-tester

Strongest counter-arguments on financial, ethical and practical grounds. Counters confirmation bias.

02

Pre-mortem analyst

It is 12 months later and the plan failed: explain why. Counters optimism.

03

Blind-spot detector

Ignore the strengths; list the biases and data gaps. Counters attachment.

04

Brutal editor

Flag every unsupported claim and logical gap. Counters fluency bias.

Caution: a model critiquing its own output shares its blind spots. Adversarial prompting widens the search; it does not replace verification against primary sources.

page 16 06 · Leadership imperatives

Three shifts for managers

  1. 01

    Protect productive friction

    Require independent first thinking on high-stakes problems. Use AI as a Socratic coach for development. Periodically test whether teams can still do core analysis without it.

  2. 02

    Redefine AI literacy

    Teach the biases by name. Anchor decision inputs to traceable data. Make stated uncertainty a standing agenda item. Match tools to risk tier.

  3. 03

    Reinvest the cognitive surplus

    Agree where saved hours go: clients, strategy, negotiation, coaching, deeper review. Measure judgment quality, not just throughput.
